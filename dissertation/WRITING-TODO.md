- **L36** — Add a Limitations section. Three items belong here that cannot be answered by the harness and should not be claimed as results: (a) cross-language parity — whether European Portuguese retrieval reaches the quality reported for English — which this work cannot measure without a parallel English corpus and gold set, and can only discuss against published multilingual benchmark figures; (b) temporal staleness — the corpus is one election cycle, and party positions move, so sysname{} answers "what the 2024 manifestos say", not "what the parties currently hold"; (c) existence questions — "does party X ever mention Y?" ask for an exhaustive search, and top-k retrieval cannot prove absence. Example: gold q-61, "O Chega menciona alguma vez touradas?". CHEGA mentions bullfighting once, as rhetoric, not policy: at 16 one may have sex-reassignment surgery but not attend an ``espectáculo tauromáquico''. Three things hide it: the chunk is classified **jovens**/**educacao**, so the analyzer's **cultura** filter excludes it; ``touradas'' and ``tauromáquico'' share no stem, so BM25 misses it; and RRF rewards agreement across lists, which a lone mention never gets. The **lexical_wide** variant (lexical list without the topic filter, synonym-seeking expansions) still ranked it 25th and moved no dev metric beyond noise (recall +0.011, nDCG +0.008, intervals spanning 0). The nuance matters too: even if retrieved, the answer must present an aside as an aside, not as a position on bullfighting. Possible fixes (future work): a mention-lookup route for existence questions — LLM-generated synonyms, BM25 over the party's whole manifesto with no topic filter, and its best hits given reserved context slots outside RRF — or an agentic search tool the generator can call. Meanwhile the refusal sentence says ``I found no answer in the excerpts I consulted'', not ``the manifesto does not say it''. Add (d) framing leakage (Section `subsec:grounding`): a grounded generator inherits the framing of its sources, the attribution rule reduces it but cannot be shown to remove it, and no automatic metric measures it (the count is by hand, on 61 questions). Its effect is uneven across parties, which matters for a tool meant to be neutral. Future work: a judge for attribution, i.e. value-laden statements made in the system's own voice. Add (e) one-sided questions: a question can be phrased in a frame that only one manifesto uses, and the answer then has a single voice. Example: gold q-47, ``Quantos imigrantes entraram em Portugal em 2022, segundo os programas?''. Only CHEGA counts arrivals (``num total de 118.000''), and it gives the figure as evidence that immigration puts pressure on housing. The other manifestos do discuss immigration (AD, BE, IL and PCP give it a section of its own), but in terms of integration, regularisation and immigrants' contribution to Social Security (BE and PS cite 2022 figures for that), and none counts arrivals. A faithful answer can only report CHEGA's number and CHEGA's reading of it. The other parties are silent on this frame, which is not a rebuttal, and a reader may take the absence of a counter-view as agreement. The attribution rule keeps the claim in CHEGA's voice (``segundo o programa do CHEGA''), and the reference says that no other manifesto gives the figure, but the asymmetry stays: the party that sets the frame of a topic owns the answer to every question asked in that frame. This is a property of the question as much as of the system, and none of the metrics flags it. Possible future work: when only one party answers, point out how the other manifestos treat the same topic. Citations to check (verify before adding to the bib): Petrocik (1996) on issue ownership; McCombs & Shaw (1972) on agenda setting; Yu et al. (2023, CREPE) and Kim et al. (2023, (QA)^2) on questions that carry presuppositions.
