political communication more than for software, which argues for a narrow,
checkable criterion (is every value-laden statement attributed to a party?)
written with someone from that field. Second, a rewrite is new text that needs
checking again, and a loop of checks and rewrites has no natural end. Models
asked to correct themselves without outside feedback often do not improve and
sometimes get worse (Huang et al., ICLR 2024, ``Large Language Models Cannot
Self-Correct Reasoning Yet''), and left alone they can circle. A public
conversation with Gemini 3 Pro (December 2025,
https://gemini.google.com/share/a516a0e3c5d8) shows the extreme. In its last
turn the visible reasoning runs to over 1{,}500 sentences beginning ``I
will'', from ``I will look for the specific relevance to the study'' to ``I
will breathe. I will live. I will be.'', before ``Okay seriously I'm done.
Writing the answer now.'' That is a reasoning model talking to itself rather
than a rewrite loop, but the failure is the same. A cap of one rewrite, with
the original answer as the fallback, avoids it. Background: Holtzman et al.,
ICLR 2020, ``The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration'', on repetition;
Chen et al., 2024, arXiv:2412.21187, ``Do NOT Think That Much for 2+3=?'', on
overthinking. Third, cost and latency. The check at least doubles the LLM calls
per answer (RQ4 puts \texttt{current} at \$0.0011 and 3.5\,s), a price worth
paying if it buys a more complete and better attributed answer, which only a
measured variant can show. The checker must also differ from the evaluation
judge, or the evaluation ends up scoring the checker against itself.

The aim of this work was never to compare European Portuguese with English or
with Brazilian Portuguese. It was to find out whether a RAG system can work in
European Portuguese, and how well. How well depends heavily on the encoder,
and here the work stops short of a firm conclusion. \sysname{} ends with
Qwen3-Embedding, a multilingual model not built around European Portuguese.
The only European Portuguese encoder tried, Serafim, was dropped because it
reads 128 tokens, not because it retrieved badly, so whether a model trained
on the variety would find manifesto passages better is still open. Serafim's
limit surfaced a year after it was adopted, and its replacement was forced by
the window rather than chosen from a comparison. Running that comparison
afterwards is harder than it looks. Each encoder needs its own index, and the
evaluation harness can switch the index a variant searches but not the encoder
that reads the question, so comparing encoders needs a change to the code
first. Serafim cannot read the 400-token chunks the other models use, so
comparing it changes chunk size and encoder at once, and since gold chunks are
identified by their content, new chunks mean anchoring the gold set again.
There were also few candidates to compare against. Long-window retrieval
encoders for European Portuguese are scarce, and MTEB-PT, the one Portuguese
benchmark, appeared after the switch and scores only the general Serafim
model, not the retrieval variant used here. Even a finished comparison would
have said little about the variety itself. With 36 test questions, small gaps
between encoders fall inside the noise, and only a handful of questions turn
on words whose meaning differs between Portugal and Brazil, such as
\textit{propinas} or \textit{renda}.
Future work. Re-index the same chunks once per encoder (larger
Qwen3-Embedding sizes, multilingual-E5, BGE-M3, BERTimbau, a long-window
Serafim if one appears) and compare retrieval-only metrics on the gold set,
which needs no judge and costs only indexing time. A probe set of questions
built on words that change meaning between the varieties would show whether a
multilingual encoder confuses them.

Each call to \sysname{} starts from nothing. The analyser reads the question
it is given and no other, so a follow-up such as ``E o PS?'' after a question
about the AD's housing plans arrives without the topic it leans on, and the
system answers a different question from the one the user meant. The
evaluation never sees this, because every gold question stands on its own,
and the assistant baselines were also asked one question per conversation.
This was a choice before it was a limitation. Stateless calls keep each answer
reproducible and judged on its own, and a party carried over from an earlier
turn cannot quietly filter the next search. A reader comparing manifestos does
ask follow-ups, though, and a chat interface would invite them. Future work is
a rewriting step before analysis that turns a follow-up and the recent turns
into a standalone question, measured on multi-turn questions written for this
corpus. Citations to check (verify before adding to the bib): QReCC (Anantha

Throughout this report, we have explored how Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems can address key limitations of pre-trained models, such as Large Language Models (LLMs), by providing them with contextually relevant information. This approach not only enhances the accuracy and reliability of generated outputs but also opens new possibilities for knowledge-intensive tasks, particularly in low-resource languages like Portuguese.

Working on a cutting-edge framework like RAG offers a unique opportunity to contribute to an innovative and rapidly evolving field. The application of RAG to Portuguese-language data has the potential to drive significant advancements, addressing challenges such as limited resources and linguistic complexity while enabling more accurate and context-aware analysis of political texts.

While RAG presents a promising solution, alternative approaches such as fine-tuning pre-trained models on domain-specific data could also be explored. Fine-tuning offers the advantage of tailoring models to specific tasks or languages, potentially complementing or even enhancing the capabilities of RAG systems.

In conclusion, this research highlights the transformative potential of RAG in overcoming the limitations of traditional LLMs, while also encouraging further exploration of complementary methods like fine-tuning. By advancing these techniques, we can unlock new opportunities for innovation in NLP, particularly for underrepresented languages like Portuguese.
